Meta · 2022 · Product Design

Redesigning internal product team pages

OverviewProblemResearchChallengeOutcome

Overview

As a product design intern, I designed a standard page template for Meta's internal search engine, so teams stopped building every page from scratch.

Role

Product Design Intern

Team

Tarjni (User Researcher)

Duration

5 weeks

Tools

Figma, Meta Design System

Status

Shipped internally

About these visuals

Meta's internal sites are confidential, so existing pages are blurred and the template is shown as wireframes.

The problem

Every team built its pages from scratch.

How might one template work for every kind of internal page?

Pages had inconsistent hierarchy and relied on custom HTML and inline CSS. Engineers were making design calls, and readers got a different experience on every page.

Research

Auditing 46 live sites

With our researcher Tarjni, I reviewed 46 internal sites and sorted every pattern into what to keep and what to remove.

Keep

  • Hero imagery and team identity
  • Cards and accordions
  • Simple top navigation

Remove

  • Walls of unbroken text
  • Layouts that broke with missing content
  • Unclear heading levels and inline CSS

Principles

Four rules for every decision

01

Company guidelines first

Only existing Meta components, so engineers had nothing new to maintain.

02

Component-driven

Cards to group content, accordions for dense documentation.

03

Modern and readable

Clear hierarchy and room to breathe between sections.

04

Accessible by default

Contrast, heading order, and keyboard navigation built in.

Key challenge

One grid for very different pages

Sites ranged from sparse team hubs to long documentation. I used a 12-column grid where each section could switch to 12, 8, or 6 columns, then tested it on the densest and sparsest pages from the audit.

Iterations

Structure before style

Three rounds over five weeks. Wireframes solved layout first, then Meta's visual system went on top.

Outcome

A shipped standard

Reflection

What I'd do differently

Test with users earlier

A few interviews in week one would have validated what the audit inferred.

Use tokens for the grid

Spacing and columns as variables would adapt as the design system changes.
