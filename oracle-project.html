The Role

Owning the system other teams build on

A motion design system defines how an interface moves: how components enter and leave the screen, how transitions are timed, and what people see while they wait. At Oracle, I owned the product strategy and roadmap for that system, which meant deciding what got standardized, in what order, and how design and engineering teams would work with it.

Two projects defined my time there: standardizing the system itself, and redesigning the loading experience across Oracle's components and templates.

Standardizing the System

One set of components, faster handoff

Motion reached the work of more than 20 teams. Before it was standardized, components were specified inconsistently, and that slowed down every handoff between design and engineering.

I drove the strategy and roadmap for standardizing the system's components and streamlined how design and engineering communicated about motion, so every team could build from the same source.

Result: design-to-dev handoff time dropped by 25% across those teams.

Redesigning the Loading Experience

Making the wait feel shorter

I directed cross-functional teams to redesign loading screens across 30+ components and 15+ templates as part of Oracle's company-wide rebranding initiative.

Result: the redesign cut perceived user latency by 40%, and the project secured Phase 2 executive funding.

Also at Oracle

Leading a Gen AI team and keeping the roadmap focused

Agentic AI app builder. I led a four-person motion design team through agile sprints to deliver 28 motion animations for the MVP of Oracle's agentic AI app builder, on a four-month timeline for leadership review.

Portfolio planning. I managed 70+ projects per quarter in Jira and Confluence. Across 7 planning cycles, I audited that work and deprecated low-impact initiatives, reducing the team backlog by 30%.
