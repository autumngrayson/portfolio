About these visuals

Oracle's work is confidential. Everything shown here is Tempo, a mock motion system I rebuilt for this portfolio using public Material Design 3 motion values.

The problem

Every team specified motion its own way.

How do we give 20+ teams one motion language that designers and engineers both use?

Timing lived in comments, meetings, or an engineer's best guess, so handoffs slowed down and the product felt inconsistent from screen to screen.

The system

One set of tokens for design and code

01

Easing curves

Seven named curves. Emphasized curves carry large moments, and standard curves handle small, frequent changes.

02

Duration scale

Sixteen steps in four bands, so short moves stay quick and full-screen changes take longer.

03

Transition patterns

Container transform, shared axis, and fade through, each built only from the tokens.

04

Handoff

Designers and engineers used the same token names, so a spec needed no translation.

Challenges

What got in the way

01

Our engineering partner was restructured

We planned to align the motion system with a company-wide engineering platform, working with a central engineering team because it seemed like the most scalable path. Midway through planning, a restructuring laid off much of that team and stalled our progress. I helped pivot to a smaller internal engineering team so we could keep building instead of waiting through several planning cycles. It taught me that setbacks matter less than how quickly a team can realign.

02

Engineering wanted a smaller library

The dev team thought the library should be a lot smaller, so we met in the middle.

120 → 50 patterns
